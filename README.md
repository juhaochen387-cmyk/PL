# 2+1D Particle Life laboratory

Open `standalone.html` directly in a modern browser. JavaScript and CSS are bundled into the file; it does not need a CDN, server, or API key. The source from the supplied archive is also included for development.

To rebuild it: `npm ci && npm run build:standalone`. Run `npm test` and `npm run lint` to check the Fock-correlation calculations and TypeScript.

## Matter-source scope

The lattice matter source inspector displays the actual spatially resolved U(1) hypercharge, eight SU(3) and three SU(2) charge/current source arrays, plus electric charge density. Those sources remain Gaussian-deposited, occupation-weighted particle proxies, including semiclassical particles outside the six-orbital Fock window. They are **not** the complete quantum matter currents. The inspector also displays anomalous particle–antiparticle bilinears calculated from the full retained Fock state; `getFockCorrelations()` exposes all normal and anomalous bilinears with fermionic signs and measured-state normalization.

The model does not define the local particle and antiparticle spinor mode functions, gauge-covariant current operators, or a regulated vacuum subtraction needed to convert all those bilinears into local Dirac currents and vacuum polarization. They are therefore not added to the gauge sources. The lattice fields are classical variables: they source the Fock evolution and receive occupation-weighted matter sources (mean-field feedback), but there is no joint quantum matter–gauge state or fully quantum back-reaction. The separate finite photon test sectors are not coupled to the live simulation. Numerical Gauss residuals do not establish quantum physical-state projection or conservation.

## Theory choice and development status

The proposed target is a **genuine 2+1D, Standard-Model-inspired, vector-like gauge theory**, not a dimensional reduction of the 3+1D Standard Model and not an implementation of the Standard Model. This choice avoids presenting the app's inherited L/R labels as intrinsic planar Weyl chirality. The live simulator is still a collection of approximate models and does not yet implement this target.

A concrete starting convention uses metric diag(+1,−1,−1), γ⁰=σ₃, γ¹=iσ₁, γ²=iσ₂; the other inequivalent irrep has −γ^μ. A four-component field may contain both irreps with identical gauge representations, but their presence alone does **not** prove anomaly cancellation: masses, regulator, counterterms and gauge representations must be specified consistently. For the plus irrep the spin rotation generator is S¹²=i[γ¹,γ²]/4=σ₃/2; physical spin is the Pauli–Lubanski scalar per unit mass for a massive one-particle representation, not a second intrinsic 2D helicity axis. The local spinor-component expectation is not generally a conserved spin quantum number for a moving packet. Antiparticles are independent creation operators in a regulated field expansion transforming in the conjugate gauge representation; their occupancy is not an automatically assigned visible-particle ID.

A possible gauge-matter content is a vector-like Dirac field for each selected SM-inspired multiplet, with both planar irreps in the same SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1) representation and vector-like covariant coupling. SU(2) doublet and singlet are internal representation labels, not 2+1D chirality; this target does not reproduce chiral SM weak interactions. The normalization of compact U(1) charges, complete spectrum, masses, Higgs potential, Yukawa terms, bare CS levels and counterterms must be fixed before a full anomaly or dynamical claim can be made. In 2+1D g² has mass dimension one.

For the isolated free two-band diagnostic the Hamiltonian is H(k)=sin(kₓa)σₓ/a+sin(kᵧa)σᵧ/a+[m+(r/a)Σᵢ(1−cos(kᵢa))]σ₃. Its corner masses are m, m+2r/a, m+2r/a, m+4r/a. The displayed occupied-band Chern number is −½ times the sum of the corner orientation times the sign of each corner mass; it is undefined at a gap closing. This is a free-band calculation in a specified orientation, **not** a complete parity-anomaly or induced non-Abelian Chern–Simons audit, and the live simulator is not evolved by this Hamiltonian. A gauge-covariant Wilson operator and regulated determinant still need implementation and validation.

The sparse Fock and link laboratories currently do not constitute a joint spatial gauge–matter state. Physical local Gauss constraints, off-diagonal current coupling and vacuum polarization, quantum Higgs and link dynamics, common-Hamiltonian reactions, measurement/decoherence and the requested interacting validation and convergence suite remain unimplemented. The existing labels and visual particles should not be interpreted as measurements of such a joint state. `npm test` checks the Clifford algebra, exhaustive four-mode CAR, and the free Wilson invariant against an independent numerical Berry-curvature integral; it does not validate the missing interacting physics.

## Experimental joint Fock foundation

`src/physics/joint/JointFockState.ts` defines a separate sparse occupation basis with globally ordered fermion modes (species, flavor, particle/antiparticle, site or momentum, spinor component, color and internal label) and multiple truncated boson modes. It provides linear creation and annihilation operators, Jordan–Wigner signs, unnormalized pure-state reduced density matrices, and mixed-state reductions via ensembles. The partial trace uses a fixed occupation-bit tensor-product convention; fermion operators spanning a cut still require Jordan–Wigner strings. A finite boson cutoff modifies the canonical commutator at its upper boundary. Sparse storage is not a guarantee against exponential growth.

An exactly solvable pair-conversion test Hamiltonian and its isolated UI demonstration exercise joint fermion–boson superpositions and Bose enhancement. This is not spatial QED and is not connected to the live gauge simulation. No gauge constraints, spatial covariant hopping, adaptive cutoffs, convergence guarantees, vacuum renormalization, or real reaction kinematics are implemented by this foundation. The live six-orbital Fock path remains unchanged.

## Isolated U(1) quantum-link test

`src/physics/joint/U1LinkTest.ts` adds a single open link between two sites to the experimental joint state. An integer register encodes truncated electric flux E=−S,…,S (it is **not** a bosonic oscillator); U raises flux, with [E,U]=U even at the cutoff, but U is not unitary at the boundary. The Hamiltonian consists of gauge-covariant fermion hopping and electric energy. Exact hopping and electric substeps are composed in a second-order split. The two local operators G₀=E−n₀+b₀ and G₁=−E−n₁+b₁ commute with both substeps. The UI example uses one fermion and a static background charge to construct a physical two-site state. The tests check local Gauss norms, gauge covariance on a generic state, norm and reverse-time evolution, including a flux cutoff edge.

This is a standalone validation sector, not the live lattice: it has no plaquette, SU(2)/SU(3), photon oscillator, spatial 2D propagation, Higgs, dynamical gauge-invariant state projection, or coupling to visible particles. It does not establish convergence as flux, lattice volume, or time-step cutoffs change. Its flux register is shared infrastructure, not a second physical gauge boson field.

## Isolated U(1) plaquette

`src/physics/joint/U1PlaquetteTest.ts` provides an open 2D square of four spin-1/2 U(1) quantum links, represented by binary link registers with E=±1/2. Its magnetic Hamiltonian is −K(P+P†), where P raises the bottom/right links and lowers the top/left links. For binary links the transition pairs are disjoint and their time evolution is exactly unitary. The link raising operators are nonunitary at the truncation edges. Electric energy ΣE² is constant for this particular spin-1/2 example.

The test computes all four local Gauss-operator norms, including optional on-site occupations and static backgrounds, and checks gauge covariance on an arbitrary state including nonphysical sectors, norm preservation and reverse-time evolution. The UI demonstrates a superposition of oppositely circulating closed flux loops. This isolated example has no dynamical matter hopping, non-Abelian links, larger lattice, continuum-limit convergence, or connection to the live simulation.

## Performance and numerical regression

The classical non-Abelian relaxation reuses the link-adjoint maps within each projection instead of recomputing link matrices on every iteration; diagnostic plaquettes also reuse links and local transformations within each diagnostic pass. The iteration count, tolerances, lattice size and physics steps are unchanged. `tests/gauge-performance-regression.ts` compares the cached divergence and repeated relaxation with a fresh-matrix reference on multiple nonzero gauge fields and sizes, including a link-field change between projections. Results may differ at floating-point roundoff level. `npx tsx benchmarks/gauge.ts` times a deterministic populated 12×12 projection; results depend on hardware and are not an overall frame-rate benchmark.

## Joint 2D U(1) matter–gauge test

`src/physics/joint/U1MatterSquare.ts` combines four site-occupation modes and four spin-1/2 U(1) link registers in one sparse joint state on an open square. Its common Hamiltonian contains gauge-covariant matter hopping, on-site masses, electric energy and a magnetic plaquette term; gauge links are not duplicated by photon oscillators. It uses a static background charge and a physical one-fermion initial state. Observables include local occupation, link flux, the full-state link-current operator expectation and Hamiltonian energy. A symmetric second-order split preserves norm and local Gauss constraints, but has nonzero finite-time-step energy error. The interactive test is lazy-loaded only when opened.

This is still a small **isolated staggered toy model**: one fermionic component per site, not the requested full Dirac fermion, antiparticle/vacuum polarization model or the live simulation. It does not supply SU(2)/SU(3) dynamics, quantum Higgs, full reactions, continuum/cutoff convergence or the remaining requirements above. Tests exercise gauge covariance for generic states, local Gauss preservation for the chosen physical state, norm/reversibility, current continuity and time-step convergence; these tests are not validation of the full theory.

### Unsplit finite-Hamiltonian evolution

The joint square demonstration also offers `evolveSquareUnsplit`, a matrix-free approximation to exp(−iHt) for the **same finite square Hamiltonian**, without Suzuki–Trotter splitting. A scaled Taylor expansion uses a conservative operator-norm bound for its series truncation; the displayed bound does not include floating-point roundoff. Evolution is not explicitly renormalized. An input safety limit prevents unbounded work, and the UI remains an isolated test. The alternative 60-step symmetric split is retained for comparison. Tests check Hermiticity across all 256 occupation-basis states, energy-expectation consistency, gauge covariance, norm and energy conservation to numerical tolerance, reverse evolution, an analytic diagonal case, and split-step convergence toward the unsplit result. These checks validate this finite model only, not the continuum limit or the missing live QFT dynamics.
